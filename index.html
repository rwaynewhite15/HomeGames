How to play Mancala

The board: six pits each, four stones in every pit, and a store at your right-hand end.

Your turn: pick one of your own pits and sow its stones one at a time, counter-clockwise. You drop a stone in your own store as you pass it, but never in your opponent's.

Go again: if your last stone lands in your own store, you take another turn.

Capture: if your last stone lands in an empty pit on your side and the pit opposite has stones, you take that stone and all the ones opposite straight into your store. A capture never also gives you another turn.

Game over: when either side is empty, the other player sweeps their remaining stones into their own store. Most stones wins.

The bots search ahead — the higher their rating, the deeper they look.

How to play Euchre

The table: four players in two partnerships. Your partner sits across from you — the player at the top of the felt. You win points together.

The deck: only 24 cards, 9 through ace. Everyone gets five, and the next card is turned face up.

The bowers — the one rule that trips people up: the jack of the trump suit (the right bower) is the highest card in the game. The other jack of the same colour (the left bower) is the second highest — and it counts as a trump, not as its printed suit. If spades are trump, the jack of clubs is a spade. You cannot follow clubs with it.

Trump order, high to low: right bower, left bower, then A K Q 10 9 of trump. Every trump beats every non-trump.

Bidding, round one: starting left of the dealer, each player may order up the turned card — making its suit trump and giving that card to the dealer, who then discards one — or pass.

Round two: if all four pass, the card is turned down and each player may name any other suit as trump, or pass again. Under Stick the Dealer the dealer cannot pass and must choose; under Standard, four more passes means a redeal.

Going alone: whoever names trump may play without their partner, who sits the hand out. Riskier, but all five tricks is worth 4 points instead of 2.

Playing: five tricks. You must follow the led suit if you hold it; otherwise play anything. Highest trump wins, or the highest card of the suit led. The winner leads the next trick.

Euchred: if the side that named trump fails to take three tricks, the other side scores 2. That is the whole tension of the game — call too greedily and you hand over points.

Game over: first partnership to 10 points.

Cards you cannot legally play are greyed out, so you can always just tap what looks best. Hands are dealt server-side and never sent to anyone else — opponents and spectators only ever see the backs.

Both halves of a winning pair are credited with the win, and ratings move against the players on the other side.

How to play Quixx

On your turn, roll all dice. Two things happen, in order:

1. White dice (everyone): Both players may mark one cell equal to the sum of the two white dice — in any row, any color cell.

2. Color dice (roller only): The roller may also mark one cell equal to a white die + a colored die, in a cell of that die's color.

Left to right: marks in a row must always be to the right of your previous marks. Skipped cells are gone for good.

Comparing the two steps: use 👁 View white-dice options during the color step to look back at what the white sum still offers (and peek ahead at the color options during the white step). Dashed cells are look-only — the step you are in is the one you can click.

Locking: the last cell needs 5+ marks in that row first. Marking it locks the row for everyone, scores a bonus ✕, and removes that color's die.

Penalty: if the roller marks nothing at all, they take −5.

Game over: 2 locked rows, or someone's 4th penalty. Most points wins.

Players: 2 to 4 seats, in any mix of people and AI. The host sets the table before hosting and can still change it in the waiting room.

The AI: each bot is just a named player with its own rating — there are no difficulty labels, because the ELO next to its name is the difficulty. They train by playing each other, so those ratings drift as they get better, and beating a highly rated one is worth more.

Scoring

Variants: Mixed Numbers scrambles each row's number order. Mixed Colors scrambles cell colors — colored dice mark cells of their color wherever they sit, and locking a row removes the die matching its last cell's color.
